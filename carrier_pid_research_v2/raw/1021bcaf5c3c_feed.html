]]> Comment on Vintage Artificial Intelligence: Before It Got Awkward by Dan Q https://blog.archive.org/2026/08/16/vintage-artificial-intelligence-before-it-got-awkward/#comment-586113 Sun, 23 Aug 2026 14:06:27 +0000 https://blog.archive.org/?p=30816#comment-586113 I’ve long loved Alter Ego, and I still dust it off every few years to have a go, but man, it hasn’t aged well! The plentiful technological and sociological changes since its release mean that it’s more… “historical” than might be ideal. But it’s still compelling, somehow, and it does spin a yarn that, somehow, does feel somewhat like a real living world around you, even when you know how the illusion is created.

I also used to love Little Computer People. And Eliza (and its clones… do you remember Creative Labs’ example in the genre, made primarily to show off the text-to-speech features of their soundcards, Dr. Sbaitso?), too: even after typing in ELIZA’s source code (remember when we had to type code from magazines?) and studying it, it’s still pretty cool.

The secret, I think, is the same as with close-up magic. If you can do just SLIGHTLY more pre-work than your audience would think feasible and have steps that are only a little more complicated than people would guess… then somehow this little extra effort tricks people into ascribing intelligence somewhere that it is not.

I guess the same explanation works for LLMs and other GenAI, too. All they really are is… statistics and random numbers! But with a complex enough process and an inconceivably-large pre-prepared dataset, the illusion of intelligence appears…
